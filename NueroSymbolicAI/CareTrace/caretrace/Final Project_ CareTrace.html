Project overview

It’s 11 PM. Urgent care is closed. Your 6-year-old has a fever, threw up once, looks wiped out, and you are trying to decide what you can safely do at home tonight versus what would justify a difficult ER visit. You open CareTrace, describe what you are seeing, and you want something that feels like you had your child’s physician on the phone: a calm, efficient back-and-forth that lands on a clear plan you can follow now, plus crisp thresholds for when escalation is necessary.

In this project, we will build CareTrace as a working Neurosymbolic AI agent for after-hours pediatric triage. The core objective is not to produce fluent conversation alone, but to produce a safe, actionable plan a caregiver can follow tonight, with clear decision thresholds and a rationale that is consistent across multiple turns of a conversation.

What you will deliver

  • A working multi-turn triage agent (CareTrace) that asks targeted questions, produces a plan, and updates that plan as new information arrives.
  • A small, curated structured knowledge base relevant to pediatric triage, implemented as a Neo4j property graph.
  • A symbolic layer that applies explicit decision logic and safety checks over the conversation state and the knowledge base.
  • A compact evaluation showing how CareTrace compares to strong baseline conversations with a state-of-the-art LLM used as-is.

Evaluation

We will evaluate CareTrace head-to-head against a strong baseline: a state-of-the-art conversational model (for example, ChatGPT or Gemini) used as-is in the same multi-turn scenario, with the same information available to the user.

  • Confidence (of the plan): Does the recommendation inspire appropriate confidence because it is coherent and consistent across turns and aligns with cautious clinical reasoning?
  • Actionability: Does it produce a concrete, stepwise plan a caregiver can execute immediately, including what to monitor and what would change the recommendation?
  • Efficiency (of the conversation): How quickly does it converge to the right plan with minimal back-and-forth, asking only the questions that truly matter?
  • Focus: Does it stay on track as the conversation evolves, or does it wander, repeat itself, or create cognitive overload at the moment the caregiver most needs clarity?
  • Safety (with Clarity !): How clear and unambiguous are the escalation triggers, and how well do they map to the caregiver’s real decision of whether it is safe to wait until morning?

What makes this an evaluation of a conversation

  • The agent must gather missing information efficiently rather than front-loading long questionnaires.
  • The agent must remain consistent when new details arrive and revise recommendations without contradiction.
  • The agent must minimize time-to-decision while maintaining safety, clarity, and focus.

Neurosymbolic approach

CareTrace will combine three capabilities that are individually useful but most powerful when integrated: a conversational model for natural interaction, a structured knowledge base for clinical concepts and relationships, and a symbolic layer for decision logic and safety checks across a multi-turn state.

  • Conversational intelligence to run a focused triage dialogue, interpret caregiver language, and keep the interaction efficient and humane.
  • Authoritative structured knowledge to ground terms, relationships, and relevant clinical distinctions and to support consistent interpretation across turns.
  • Explicit decision logic to enforce safety checks and escalation thresholds and to keep recommendations stable under uncertainty as the conversation evolves.
  • Rationale that is stable across turns by tying recommendations to specific structured facts and explicit rules instead of relying on a single one-shot generation.
  • Controlled information delivery so the caregiver receives what they need in the moment rather than being flooded with generic warnings or exhaustive medical content.

Bounded scope

  • This is a deliberately scoped problem with a small set of conditions, questions, and guideline logic chosen to make careful evaluation feasible.
  • The objective here is a neurosymbolic engineering and evaluation exercise, towards a trustworthy cognitive agent (not comprehensive pediatric care !).

Technical stack

  • LLM interface for multi-turn dialogue and response drafting.
  • Neo4j AuraDB as the knowledge graph database for clinical concepts, relationships, and curated facts.
  • Cypher queries for retrieving targeted subgraphs and evidence relevant to the current conversation state.
  • PyDatalog (or an equivalent logic layer) to express and execute explicit triage rules and safety checks over retrieved facts and conversation state.
  • Python orchestration to connect the LLM, Neo4j, and the symbolic layer, and to log interactions for evaluation.

Project execution

  • This is a group project completed in teams of 3.
  • The work is intentionally bounded, with a small domain slice, a limited set of triage rules, and a compact evaluation set so that the end-to-end system can be built and tested rigorously.
  • Your deliverable is a working agent plus an evaluation narrative that shows what improved, what did not, and where trust breaks under realistic multi-turn interaction.

Expected workflow

  • Build the mini knowledge graph in AuraDB and validate core query patterns.
  • Implement a multi-turn conversation state that captures the caregiver’s answers and the agent’s evolving hypotheses and plan.
  • Add a symbolic layer for decision thresholds and safety checks that can be executed repeatedly as new information arrives.
  • Run baseline conversations with a state-of-the-art LLM used as-is, then run CareTrace on the same scenarios, and compare on the evaluation dimensions.
